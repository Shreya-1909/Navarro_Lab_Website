Injectable Gels 3D Bioprinting Aneurysm Therapy

Catheter-Injectable Gels for Drug Delivery to the Beating Heart

Cardiovascular therapies after myocardial infarction often rely on systemic delivery, requiring repeated dosing and leading to off-target effects and rapid clearance. These limitations significantly hinder clinical translation.

Challenge: Rapid diffusion from the infarct zone and loss of therapeutic bioactivity due to the mechanically active myocardium.

We are developing an injectable nanoparticle-crosslinked hydrogel that integrates dynamic covalent chemistry to enable shear-thinning, self-healing, and catheter delivery.

  • Nanoparticles act as multifunctional crosslinkers
  • Dynamic covalent bonds enable extrusion and retention
  • Reservoir-based sustained therapeutic delivery

Drug-loaded nanoparticles synthesized from poly(L-lactic acid) derivatives are used as crosslinkers for elastin-like protein networks.

Gels are characterized using rheology, cytocompatibility assays, release kinetics, and evaluated in preclinical myocardial infarction models.

This platform enables localized, sustained delivery of proteins, growth factors, and gene therapies while minimizing off-target effects.

Funding: This project was selected for NIH K99-R00 support.

3D-Bioprinted Cardiovascular Tissue Models for Rapid Drug Screening

Motivation

Drug-induced cardiotoxicity is a leading cause of clinical drug failure. With reduced reliance on animal testing, scalable and physiologically relevant in vitro models are urgently needed. Traditional bioinks lack the balance between printability, stability, and cellular support required for high-throughput cardiovascular screening.

Research Strategy

We are developing viscoelastic bioinks that combine static and dynamic crosslinking chemistries to achieve ideal extrusion, shape fidelity, and post-printing stability. Human induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes from diverse populations are incorporated to capture sex- and ethnicity-specific drug responses.

Experimental Approach

Recombinant collagen and hyaluronic acid are chemically modified to enable controlled dynamic and light-induced static crosslinking. Bioinks are optimized for mechanical properties, print fidelity, and long-term tissue function, enabling scalable drug screening platforms.

Broader Impact

This platform can be extended to model other tissues such as brain and liver, improving accessibility and affordability through sustainable polymer design while enabling more inclusive and predictive drug testing.

In Situ Gelling Polymers for Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm Therapy

Motivation

Abdominal aortic aneurysm is a life-threatening condition with limited treatment options. Current interventions do not promote regeneration and may fail over time. A minimally invasive, regenerative therapy is needed to stabilize aneurysms before rupture.

Research Strategy

Our lab is designing in situ gelling copolymers that rapidly crosslink upon contact with native biomolecules—without light, catalysts, or external triggers. The material occludes the aneurysm while providing mechanical support and promoting neovascularization.

Experimental Approach

Degradable copolymers are synthesized using radical polymerization strategies that introduce hydrolytically cleavable backbone units and highly reactive pendant groups. The materials are delivered as viscous liquids and evaluated in preclinical aneurysm models.

Broader Impact

This platform can be adapted for brain aneurysms, wound healing, surgical adhesives, and other regenerative applications, extending sustainable chemistry strategies to a wide range of biomedical materials.

Funding & Collaboration

This research positions the lab for competitive funding from NIH, NSF, DoD, and private foundations such as the American Heart Association. The work is highly collaborative, integrating expertise in chemistry, biomaterials, nanotechnology, biomechanics, and cardiovascular medicine across engineering and medical disciplines.
